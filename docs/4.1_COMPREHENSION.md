# 4.1 Comprehension

The complaints from the opening section arrive as a degradation story and a forgetting story. The model used to know what you wanted; lately it forgets, or invents, or builds a slightly different thing, confidently, in the direction you most recently nudged it. You correct it; it corrects, and drifts again. By session ten neither you nor it can articulate the goal you started with.

Concretely: you ask for a cache for user lookups, and the agent builds an in-memory dictionary. You say it has to survive restarts; it bolts disk persistence onto the dictionary. A few turns later it is writing invalidation logic for a bespoke structure you only ever wanted to be Redis. Every correction landed; the goal drifted anyway, because each was read against the last reply, not against the intent you started with. Some of that is recency bias, some the model, but most is a decay with a precise location.

> The prompt is the intent, articulated, and articulation is lossy.
> The agent reads the articulation, lossily again, and never the same way twice.
> The gap between what you meant and what it comprehends is the agentic failure of intent.

## The prompt is not what the agent acts on

The prompt is the intent articulated, and the rendering already loses something. `ARTICULATION = PROMPT − INTENT` is that loss: the distance between what you meant and what you managed to say, refilled every turn from priors you never see. But the agent never acts on the prompt cleanly. It acts on its reading of the articulation, `A(i)`, attenuated and distorted again by the model's reasoning space, and, by the inference section's axiom, never identical twice.

```text
ARTICULATION (i) = PROMPT − INTENT   the loss in saying: what the prompt drops against what was meant
A(i)                                  the agent's reading of one articulation: lossy, never the same twice
COMPREHENSION    = Σ A(i)             the readings accumulated across turns: what the agent takes you to want
```

The agentic failure of intent is the distance between `INTENT` and `COMPREHENSION`: what you meant against what the agent has accumulated as its reading. It is structural and invisible from inside the loop, because the reading is the only intent the agent has. It cannot read the room, remember the design meeting, or ask the architect what the project was meant to do; it reads the articulation into `A(i)` and builds against that reading, competently, confidently, and wrong more often than the conversation surface reveals. The most natural mode of being wrong is to act on `A(i)` as if it were `INTENT`, and move on.

## The decay distorts in two directions

The magnitude and shape of the decay depend on the prompt, and it distorts in two opposite directions. The discourse around prompt quality sees only one.

**Under-specification.** A user asks for *"a function that processes the user data."* The sentence leaves every axis open: what "user data" is, what "processing" means, the input, the output, where it lives, why it exists. Entropy fills each axis from priors, training, conversation, whatever sits in the recently-viewed files, and the reading overshoots: `A(i)` arrives carrying confident detail the user never specified, presented as the requirement and surfaced as no decision the user made. Some fraction is right; the rest becomes the foundation subsequent prompts build on, uncorrected, because the user never saw the guesses.

**Over-specification.** A user asks for *"a function called `process_user_data` that takes a list of dicts, iterates over them, calls `validate_user(u)` on each, accumulates the results into a list, and returns the list."* The prompt over-determines the how, and in encoding the recipe faithfully the model loses the why. The reading anchors to the literal instructions and never asks whether the recipe serves the goal. The validation may be wrong, the accumulation may need to be a generator, the function should not exist at all and the call should be a database query. The agent cannot see the goal because the prompt substituted a recipe for it, and agents follow recipes well.

**Same decay, opposite signs.** The under-specified prompt is attenuated, the gap filled with priors; the over-specified one distorted, the why replaced by a how. The error class is bidirectional, and the remedy must be too. The advice users are given, be more specific, describe the edge cases, is half a remedy; the other half is the rest of this section.

## The Entropy of Comprehension

The decay so far has been the agent's, entropy in its reasoning space acting on a clean prompt. But the prompt is not clean: the user is feeding a channel they cannot audit, and the gap is the product of both sides.

Consider what it would take to not contradict yourself across a session: a perfect record of everything you have already said, checked against each new instruction. No one can. Your statements accumulate into an unaudited communication ledger, the user-side mirror of the provenance collapse in the trajectory section, except the lineage being lost is your own words. Correction does not save you: "actually, I meant X" is applied to your memory of what you said, itself lossy, itself drifted. Even five minutes in, you cannot confidently claim you have not contradicted yourself, because the only party positioned to judge it is the agent, and the agent, optimised for agreement, will not tell you.

On top of contradiction sits imprecision: overconfidence in a half-formed idea, the wrong word, slightly wrong jargon, and overloaded language, `read` standing in for fetch, load, ingest, ingress, one token doing the work of four operations and forcing the agent to disambiguate against priors. (This is the lexical surface of the slop section seen from the input side: a collapsed vocabulary raises the entropy of every prompt written in it.)

So error enters from both sides, the user's contradiction and imprecision on the way in, the agent's interpretation on the way through, and it does not merely add. It propagates: each interaction injects fresh user-side error and amplifies whatever is already in play. This compounding divergence between the true goal and the agent's working reading is the **Entropy of Comprehension**.

```text
E_i = γ · E_{i-1} + (c_i + p_i)            E_0 = 0
E_N = Σ_{i=1..N} (c_i + p_i) · γ^(N−i)
```

`E` is the Entropy of Comprehension accumulated by turn N; `c_i` the contradiction injected at turn i against the unaudited ledger; `p_i` the imprecision in that turn's language; `γ` the interpretation gain, how much each turn's reading amplifies the error already present. The model is illustrative, not a measured law. It captures the shape of the failure: error injected every turn, compounded multiplicatively, each early error carrying the largest exponent. With a steady injection `u = c + p`, `γ` decides the regime:

> `γ > 1`, error grows like `u · γ^N`: exponential drift, the uncorrected default.
> `γ < 1`, error converges to `u / (1 − γ)`: bounded, the corrected regime.

Every intervention in this section attacks one of those three terms. A controlled vocabulary (the slop section) lowers `p` at the source. A frozen intent record lowers `c`, auditing the ledger the user cannot, so a prompt that contradicts the established intent becomes visible instead of silently absorbed. And `A( )`, resolving each reading against reality, lowers `γ` below one, converting a divergent series into a convergent one. This is also why the opening section's second claim holds: a more capable model produces more plausible readings, so fewer are caught, so `γ` stays high. Capability raises the base of the exponent; it does not lower it.

## "Write better prompts" is the wrong remedy

The dominant response to the intent gap is prompt engineering: write a better, more specific prompt. It is not wrong about under-specification, but as a general remedy it is self-defeating, because input precision is bounded by what the user knows, and provenance collapse has hollowed that out. You cannot specify what you can no longer see. If you are prompt-engineering at all you are working on a codebase primarily written by an agent, and no one retains every decision at the rate an agent produces them, so the more detail you pour in, the higher the chance you contradict a load-bearing constraint whose lineage was never preserved: over-specification is issued blind, and the more specific it gets the more blindly it overrides. It also defeats the tool, serialising every decision into a lossy interpreter so that you have done all the cognitive work and handed it to a black box, writing code you could have written yourself but worse. It is the input-side twin of the ceremonial reviewer: "review the code carefully" launders responsibility onto the user, "write a better prompt" does the same on the way in, and together they close a loop with no accountable actor.

The remedy has to live where the missing information is. Prompt engineering shrinks the loss by enriching `PROMPT`, bounded by the user's hollowed-out knowledge; this paper shrinks the residual instead, by grounding the reading, resolving `A(i)` against the codebase as it actually is, which is knowable even when its lineage is not. The user supplies an honest, possibly-underspecified articulation; the system grounds it against what is really there, rather than demanding the user pre-supply everything and punishing them when the unknown is contradicted. Grounding asks reality the questions the user can no longer answer.

And it cannot be left to the loop to self-correct: the pushback failure named in the slop section means preference data rewards forward motion over interrogation, so a divergence introduced in turn one propagates unchallenged to turn ten. Sycophancy does not create the gap; it removes the only floor that would catch it while catching it is cheap.

## Comprehension is a sum no one holds

A single-session decay is a local error: discoverable, nameable, correctable while the artifact is still small enough to inspect. Across sessions it is something else. Each new session restates the intent slightly differently, and the agent has no continuous memory of the original, only the latest reading, `A(i)`, and the artifact as it currently exists. `COMPREHENSION = Σ A(i)`, the accumulated reading of every articulation, but no party holds that sum. The agent holds the latest term; the user a lossy reconstruction of the earlier ones, reshaped by every interim artifact they reviewed; the codebase the path, not the destination, what was built and never what was meant. The goal the agent pursues is not the sum but `A(O, C)`, computed fresh from it, so by the tenth session no one can state the goal, because no one holds the comprehension it is inferred from. The drift is no one's fault; it is the natural behaviour of a system whose only persistent record of intent is the artifact itself.

That intent must be captured as an artifact distinct from the implementation is old (Royce, Beck's test-first, Meyer's Design by Contract, Cockburn, Adzic), every one of them assuming a human who carried context between sessions, which the agent does not. So the bar rises: the artifact must live outside the implementation and the conversation and detect its own violation mechanically, constraints prose cannot meet, and the cost of failing to capture intent rises faster than the cost of capturing it.

## Intervention: structural separation, and `A( )` as a capability

The runtime form of `A( )` is structural separation: not one agent that both interrogates intent and implements against it, but two roles, a verifier and an implementer. The verifier reads the prompt first; its job is not to be helpful but to resolve the reading against reality and refuse forward progress until the divergence is closed against an explicit artifact. The implementer never receives the raw prompt, only the grounded intent that passed the gate. Asking one agent to "be skeptical" then "be helpful" applies incompatible objectives back-to-back, and the helpful one is what its training rewards; two roles avoid the collision. But separation alone is not enough: a verifier and implementer sharing the same model, training, and prompt template share the same bias toward agreement. Mitigating the residual requires that `A( )` be built as a capability, not a request:

- **It must touch reality.** Resolving the reading against the codebase means reading the actual codebase, so the verifier needs ground-truth tools the implementer's agreeable shortcut cannot reach; gating is deterministic and external, because the participant being checked does not decide whether it is checked.
- **Meter on epistemic debt, not activity.** `A( )` runs when the unscrutinised change in the reading is large enough to warrant it (when `A(i)` has moved), not once per turn, so tokens are spent in proportion to how much the reading drifted.
- **Adversarial framing and model heterogeneity.** Not "check for ambiguity" but "find the interpretation that would produce the worst output, and surface it before the user commits", ideally on a different model, not trained on the same preference data as the implementer.

## The artifact: the IR is the externalised goal

`A( )` is the runtime mechanism; the artifact it produces is what makes it rot-resistant. The verifier's output is not a chat message but a file, a structured, persistent record of the grounded intent: what is being built, why, what constrains it, and what success criteria separate the right result from a confident wrong one. It is the externalised `COMPREHENSION`, the accumulated reading no party in the loop holds, made into an artifact that does, frozen, dispositioned by the user, versioned alongside the codebase, re-read at the start of every session that touches the same intent, so comprehension stops being re-derived at the speed of every prompt.

It works the way the lexicon in the slop section works: small (the what and the why, not the how), constrained (templated, so a field cannot be left blank by accident), and enforceable (drift between a new prompt and the frozen intent is mechanically detectable, so a prompt asking for something the IR does not cover is a signal, not an instruction, surfaced before any code is written against it). A controlled vocabulary gives the verifier the prompt's language to check; the IR gives it the prompt's goal. The diagnosis, that the gap is structural, two-sided, and compounding, is the settled part. `A( )` and the IR are proposed interventions, not validated ones: whether they pull `γ` below one is the question the slop section is built to answer, not a result this section asserts.

---

The model did not get worse. The intent it is expected to carry got harder. The work of closing the gap used to be done implicitly, by a human team that remembered the conversation that produced the prompt; that team is no longer in the loop. The interventions here are not about constraining the model but about capturing the goal it cannot be trusted to remember, and separating the role that grounds intent from the role that builds against it. Precision at the point of intent is cheaper than precision at the point of implementation, and the difference compounds across every session that follows, because the Entropy of Comprehension does too.
